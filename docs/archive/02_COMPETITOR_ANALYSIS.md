# Adaptive Core — Competitor Analysis

## 1. Competitive frame

Adaptive Core competes with any product or behavior that answers “what core workout should I do today?” This includes fixed abs challenges, home-workout libraries, broad personalized fitness subscriptions, strength trackers, minimalist counters, free videos, and self-written routines.

Named products discussed during discovery included Six Pack in 30 Days, Home Workout, BetterMe, MadMuscles, BodBot, Fitbod, Nike Training Club, Freeletics, Hevy, Caliber, JEFIT, Strong, P4P Abify, 200 Sit Ups Pro, Daily Ab Workout, and minimalist counters such as OneCrunches. This list is a category map, not a claim that every product was audited to the same depth.

## 2. Category comparison

| Category | Why users choose it | Structural strength | Common gap relevant to Adaptive Core |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30-day abs challenge | Clear promise and simple calendar | Immediate comprehension | Difficulty tied to day, terminal program, transformation claims |
| Exercise library | Choice and demonstrations | Content breadth | User must program their own progression |
| Broad personalized subscription | Polish and variety | Brand, content, onboarding | Opaque personalization, breadth, price, complexity |
| Strength tracker | Records and overload visibility | Strong progress history | Manual logging and advanced-user bias |
| Minimal counter | Speed and low friction | Simplicity | No balanced program, adaptation, or recovery |
| Free video/self-programming | No app commitment | Free and familiar | No closed feedback loop or persistent state |

## 3. Competitor strengths to respect

Adaptive Core should not assume competitors are weak. Existing products demonstrate that users value:

- a clear first action;
- polished exercise demonstrations;
- minimal setup;
- familiar “day” or level language;
- records and visible consistency;
- broad catalogs;
- recognizable brands and social proof.

The MVP should learn from these strengths while remaining narrow.

## 4. Gaps and responses

### Claimed personalization without visible causality

**Gap:** A plan may be labeled personalized even when it is selected once during onboarding or progresses on a fixed calendar.

**Response:** Every completed workout updates stored state, and meaningful changes include a short explanation such as “Volume stayed stable because the last session felt hard.”

### Fixed difficulty curve

**Gap:** The next day advances even after failure or high perceived effort.

**Response:** Use hold, small progression, recovery, and regression outcomes. Calendar position never overrides user response.

### Variety without programming control

**Gap:** Random novelty can overload the same pattern or prevent mastery.

**Response:** Select from eligible exercises only after movement balance, recent exposure, fatigue, exclusions, and level are applied.

### Program completion cliff

**Gap:** A challenge ends with no meaningful next phase.

**Response:** There is no terminal day. Capacity state continues, with periodic reassessment only when useful.

### Friction and trust loss

**Gap:** Ads interrupt sessions, settings reset, or purchase terms feel unstable.

**Response:** Protect the workout flow, persist preferences, state terms clearly, and preserve entitlements.

### Content-first economics

**Gap:** High-quality libraries can require significant production or licensing expense.

**Response:** Launch with a small, quality-controlled, commercially licensed set of lightweight demonstrations and critical cues. Invest in proprietary content only after validation or revenue.

## 5. Positioning

### Category

Adaptive core progression for short at-home sessions.

### Core promise

> Your next workout is built from how the last one actually went.

### Supporting proof

- feedback changes subsequent programming;
- missed days recalculate rather than break a calendar;
- users can see why load changed;
- movement patterns are balanced behind a simple interface;
- pain or unsuitable movements are excluded rather than repeatedly served.

### What not to lead with

- “AI coach”;
- the number of exercises;
- a guaranteed six-pack;
- “30 days”;
- technical phrases such as programmatic builder;
- future full-body scope.

## 6. Defensibility thesis

The early moat is not an uncopyable feature. It is the quality of the complete learning system:

- structured exercise metadata;
- progression and regression graphs;
- deterministic decisions that can be improved safely;
- feedback data tied to generated prescriptions;
- trust earned through transparent behavior;
- a focused loop with fewer competing priorities.

Over time, anonymized aggregate outcomes may improve defaults, but the MVP must work without large datasets or machine learning.

## 7. Competitive feature decisions

| Feature seen in the market | MVP decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed daily calendar | Reject | Conflicts with performance-led adaptation |
| Large exercise catalog | Limit | Quality and metadata matter more than count |
| AI chat coach | Exclude | Cost, safety, and weak fit with core thesis |
| Camera form analysis | Exclude | High technical and safety complexity |
| Health/wearable sync | Defer | Valuable but not required to prove adaptation |
| Manual workout editor | Defer | Transfers complexity to beginner |
| Simple effort feedback | Include | High value, low friction, central input |
| Pain/replacement flow | Include | Safety and real-life flexibility |
| Progress records | Include minimally | Makes progress visible |
| Full game economy | Defer | Must not distract from the core loop |
| Social feed/challenges | Exclude | High scope and moderation cost |

## 8. Competitor audit template for pre-launch refresh

Before store launch, refresh volatile facts for the top direct competitors:

- store and region;
- current price, trial, renewal language, and lifetime option;
- first-run onboarding length;
- whether post-workout feedback changes a later workout;
- missed-day behavior;
- progression explanation;
- exercise replacement and pain handling;
- session interruptions;
- demonstration format and licensing quality;
- latest recurring positive and negative review themes.

This refresh verifies the positioning; it does not reopen settled MVP scope without material evidence.
